Decision rule, re-run

My first pass asked "where does one boolean go?" — with that framing (iii) loses cleanly and (ii) wins. Your screenshot reframes it as "has the collection outgrown its container?", and the honest answer changes, because the surviving non-overlap is no longer tidiness:

What a panel doesCan the strip already do it?Survives?
Give the picker its real estate back No — the strip is the cost. Structural. ✓ yes
Reachable with a workspace focused No — viewFor renders the picker only when nothing is focused. Menu mirror is a workaround. ✓ yes
Grouping + a help line per setting No — a flat unlabelled row has no room. ✓ yes
Consolidate settings for their own sake — ✗ cut — that's M14's job, not a reason on its own

Revised verdict (a): option (iii-b) — a minimal ⌘, Settings panel, and migrate the 3 existing controls into it

Three items survive the rule, and the first is the one I'd previously missed entirely: the picker gets its real estate back. That's a benefit to the surface you touch on every project open, not a home for one new toggle — which is why (ii) now reads as the wrong call. (ii) would have made your screenshot worse.

Migrating the 3 controls is what makes it worth doing. Option (iii-a) — panel with only the new gate — is the trap: it builds the surface, leaves the strip intact, and spreads settings across four places. Either move them or don't build the panel.

M14 relationship changes from "defer" to "absorb." M14 becomes an extension of a panel that exists (project-list management, hotkeys) rather than a migration of controls that kept accumulating in the wrong place. Its scope line also needs a correction regardless: it lists "menu-bar / PiP visibility toggles" and "yolo-mode toggle" as future work, but both shipped in M5/M6.

Scope guard for the build. Panel, ⌘,, Settings… menu item, 4 groups, migrate 3 controls, gate as the 4th. Not in scope: project-list management, hotkey editor, search/filter in settings, per-project settings, or moving PiP mode out of the View menu.

Bookkeeping consequence

This is a bigger build than the verdict I first wrote, so WP2 needs honest re-sizing rather than quietly absorbing it: WP2 goes S → M, gaining a panel task and a migration task. WP1 stays a pure probe — decided here, built in WP2, exactly as you asked.
